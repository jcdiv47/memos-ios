Design proposal · iOS 26 and later · October 2026

Memos for iPhone

A native client for self-hosted Memos. Your notes stay on paper. The controls are glass that floats above them.

  • Scroll the timeline. Memos pass under the glass title row and the bottom bar, and the glass picks up their color.
  • Tap “Capture a thought…” and write a memo. Tags you type with # turn blue, and Save adds the memo to the top of the timeline.
  • Tap “All Spaces” under the title to switch Space, or to switch between your memos and everyone’s. Choose a Space, then capture, and the composer offers to share the memo with that Space.

The rule

Paper for what you wrote, glass for what you do

Memos is a stream of short notes. Most of the time the screen should be those notes, at a comfortable reading size, on the same warm paper the web app uses. Liquid Glass is reserved for the layer above: the title row, the bottom bar, menus and sheets. Because the controls float over the timeline, the screen keeps its full height, and the content tints the glass as it scrolls underneath.

This split decides almost every case. When something is a memo, an attachment, a tag or a list row, it is drawn opaque. When it is a control that moves, collapses or morphs, it uses system glass. We use the system components wherever they exist and apply .glassEffect by hand only in a few places, listed below.

Glass

  • The bottom bar: Library, the capture field and Search
  • Toolbar buttons and the Space title menu
  • Composer sheet at partial height
  • The send button (the only tinted glass)
  • Reaction picker and map controls

Never glass

  • Memo cards, comments, link previews
  • Tag text, task checkboxes, reaction counts
  • Photos and attachment thumbnails
  • Anything inside a glass surface (no glass on glass)
  • The editor’s writing area
Key screens

Capture, read, find

The timeline above is the home screen. These three cover the rest of the core loop.

Information architecture

Where each part of the web app lands

There is no tab bar. The timeline is home, Library is one tap away at the bottom left, and Search is at the bottom right. Explore and Spaces both live in the title menu, because each one narrows the same timeline instead of being a place of its own.

Decided · Layout

One glass row at the top, one at the bottom

The top row holds the title with its Space menu, plus the filter and Inbox buttons. The bottom row holds Library, the capture field and Search. A tab bar with only Memos and Library would have cost a second bottom row for little gain, so Library becomes a pushed screen. Both rows keep the same height while you scroll, which leaves most of the screen for memos.

Decided · Explore

Explore is a filter on the timeline, not a tab

On the web, Explore is already one half of a Home / Explore toggle in the Space switcher, and it means every creator whose memos you can read, you included. On an instance where nobody else’s memos are readable to you, Explore shows exactly what Home shows. A tab for it would be a duplicate for most self-hosters.

The app calls ListUserStats with parent users/-. It returns one entry for each creator with at least one memo you can read, and any signed-in user can call it. If the only entry is you, the Mine / Everyone toggle is hidden. This also covers team instances where everyone else keeps their memos private, which a user count would miss. The app checks again on refresh, so the toggle appears once a teammate shares their first memo.

Decided · Inbox

Inbox stays a toolbar bell on every instance

Inbox doesn’t get a place in the bottom bar. Most people check it only when the badge shows something new, and a badge on a bell gives that signal without taking room from capture. Keeping it in one place on every instance also means the layout never changes when a teammate starts sharing.

Decided · Default visibility

A new memo never starts more open than you chose

Each memo starts from your default in Settings, the same memo_visibility setting the web uses, and Private if it was never set. Writing into a Space can only narrow that default. The app never widens it on its own, and it doesn’t remember the last visibility you picked.

When you captureThe memo starts asWhy
With “All Spaces” selectedYour defaultThe memo isn’t placed in a Space, so your setting applies as-is. Changing it on iPhone changes it on the web too.
Inside a Space, default PrivatePrivate, with a “Share with Studio” chipPlacing a memo in a Space doesn’t share it. One tap switches it to Space visibility.
Inside a Space, default Protected or PublicSpaceOnly the Space’s members can read it. Writing in a team’s Space shouldn’t publish to the whole instance by accident. You can widen it with one tap.

After removing the Space from a draftPrivate, if it was SpaceSpace visibility means nothing without a Space. The web does the same.

One difference from the web: today the web editor keeps your default when you pick a Space, so a Public default stays Public inside Studio. The iPhone rule narrows it to Space. For now the rule applies on iPhone only. Bringing it to the web is listed under follow-ups at the end.

Decided · Offline

Your own memos work on a plane

The app keeps a full copy of every memo you wrote, archived ones included. Reading, writing and searching your own memos don’t need a connection. Other people’s memos and full-size attachments are cached as you view them. Personal memo collections are small: 1,284 memos is a few megabytes of text.

Works offline

  • Reading and searching all your memos, including tags and saved views
  • Capturing, editing, pinning, archiving and deleting
  • Checking off tasks
  • Voice memos, transcribed on the device
  • Adding photos and files, which upload later
  • Everyone’s memos you’ve already seen, shown read-only

Needs a connection

  • Comments and reactions
  • Share links and Space invitations
  • Searching other people’s memos
  • Full-size attachments you haven’t opened before
  • Link previews for new links

The line: anything you do to your own memos queues and uploads later. Anything that reaches another person waits until you’re online, so nobody receives a comment hours after you thought you sent it.

Sync stepHow
First syncPage through ListMemos with your user as creator, 1,000 memos per page, normal and archived.
ChangesOn launch, foreground and pull-to-refresh, fetch updated_ts >= timestamp(…), ordered by update_time asc. The window starts one second before the last sync, because the server stores seconds. Duplicates are matched by memo name.
DeletionsThe server keeps no record of deleted memos, so a change fetch can’t see them. Once a day on Wi-Fi, and on pull-to-refresh, the app compares its memo list with the server’s and drops the ones that are gone. Opening a memo that returns “not found” removes it immediately.
CreatingThe app picks each new memo’s ID itself (memo_id), so it can retry an upload safely. If the server says the ID already exists, the earlier attempt went through.
Upload orderChanges upload in the order you made them. Attachments upload before the memo that uses them.
AttachmentsThumbnails for your memos download in the background. Full-size files are cached as you open them, up to 500 MB, oldest first out. A “Keep all attachments” switch in Settings downloads everything for travel.
Signing outDeletes the local copy and the queue. If any changes haven’t uploaded yet, a confirmation says how many.
Conflicts

Text is never silently lost

The server accepts any update without checking whether the memo changed since you last saw it. So before uploading an edit, the app fetches the memo and compares its update_time with the version the edit started from.

Changes to pin, archive, visibility or Space simply apply, and the last one wins. If the memo’s text changed on both sides, the phone’s version is saved as a new memo that references the original. A banner on both says “Edited on another device. Your version was saved separately.” You then merge them by hand.

Offline stateWhat you see
No connectionThe subtitle under the title reads “All Spaces · Offline”. There’s no banner, because everything you need still works.
Waiting to uploadA small clock replaces the visibility icon in that memo’s metadata row, and VoiceOver reads it as “Waiting to sync”.
Everyone, offlineShows cached memos with “Updated 2 hours ago” at the top of the list.
SearchYour own memos match instantly from the local copy, online or not. When online, other people’s matches appear below them as they arrive.

Beyond the app

System surfaces

Most memos start outside the app. These are the places capture can begin.

App icon

Built in Icon Composer with two layers: a paper base and the Memos mark as a glass foreground. That covers the default, dark, clear and tinted appearances.

Widgets

Quick capture (small), Pinned (medium) and On this day (large). Glyphs use .widgetAccentable() so they hold up in clear and tinted modes.

Controls

New memo and Voice memo for Control Center, the Lock Screen and the Action button, built as ControlWidgets that call App Intents.

App Intents

Create memo, Append to memo and Search memos. Memos are an IndexedEntity, so Spotlight and Shortcuts can find them.

Share extension

Save a link, some text or photos as a memo. Link previews come from the server’s GetLinkMetadata.

iPad

A NavigationSplitView. The sidebar holds Memos, the Library sections, saved views and Spaces, and the timeline fills the detail column.

Accessibility

Glass that holds up under every setting

Reduce Transparency

System glass frosts on its own. Our hand-placed glass has to stay legible when frosted, so the capture field placeholder uses Ink 2, never a lighter gray.

Increase Contrast

Glass gets a defined border. Tags are also underlined, so they don’t rely on color alone.

Dynamic Type

Cards reflow up to AX5. The metadata row wraps under the content, and the capture field grows taller instead of truncating.

Reduce Motion

Zoom transitions become cross-fades. Morphs stay but have no bounce.

VoiceOver

A memo card is one element with custom actions: Pin, Archive, React, Copy link. A rotor jumps between tags.

Build notes

What the design assumes

PlatformDeployment target iOS 26, the first Liquid Glass release, so there is no fallback styling to maintain. SwiftUI throughout, Swift 6 language mode, Observation for state.
APIA Connect-Swift client generated by buf from proto/api/*.proto, the same Connect transport the web client uses. The access token is stored in the Keychain and refreshed the same way the web client does it.
Sign-inPassword through AuthService. SSO through IdentityProviderService and ASWebAuthenticationSession.
OfflineA full local copy of your own memos, plus a queue of changes waiting to upload. See “Offline” above for the sync and conflict rules.
RenderingA native Markdown renderer for what memos actually contain: task lists, code, quotes, links and tables. Checking a task rewrites the memo content, as it does on the web.
VoiceRecord on device and send to AIService.Transcribe when the instance has AI enabled. Otherwise fall back to on-device speech recognition.
Not in v1Instance administration, user management, webhooks, the Apple Watch app and Live Activities.
Later

Server and web follow-ups

None of these block the iPhone app. Each would make it simpler or more consistent.

  1. Keep a record of deleted memos. The show_deleted field already exists but is ignored. If the server kept deleted-memo records, a change fetch could see deletions and the daily full comparison could go.
  2. Reject updates to a changed memo. An UpdateMemo check on update_time or an etag would let the server catch text conflicts. The fetch-and-compare before each upload could then go.
  3. Space default on the web. Deferred for now: on iPhone, writing into a Space narrows a Protected or Public default to Space, while the web editor keeps your default.
